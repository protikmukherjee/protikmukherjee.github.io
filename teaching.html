Philosophy

How I teach

I prioritize clarity, iteration, and respect for the different disciplinary starting points students bring. My goal is for them to leave each course able to read a problem space and its audience, make deliberate platform and tool choices, build and test working prototypes, and defend their decisions in clear, decision-oriented language.

Short briefs, fast iteration. Every unit is anchored in a small, concrete brief with a minimum-viable artifact due early, followed by a structured iteration cycle.

Interaction-first design. I frame platform choices, web, game, ambient/IoT, mobile, or embedded, as consequences of audience and interaction intent, not technology-first defaults.

User testing as a habit. Moderated testing with at least one external participant per project. Students learn to write a test plan, synthesize findings, and act on them.

Critique culture. Rubric-aligned, peer-and-instructor critique as the default classroom mode, with the psychological safety students need to present imperfect drafts.

Online and hybrid readiness. Comfortable on D2L Brightspace (TMU) and Quercus Canvas (UTM). I keep a predictable weekly cadence, objectives post, demo, practice brief, wrap-up of common issues, and hold office hours with explicit agendas.

Assessment with process evidence. Authentic, reasoning-based tasks where students must justify platform and interaction choices. Design briefs, testing plans, and iteration logs make learning visible and make shortcuts low-value. Every comment I leave names what's working, what isn't, and one concrete next step.

Equity in practice. Transparent expectations and rubrics, multiple pathways to demonstrate learning, examples students can map to their own backgrounds, jargon named when used.
